1.  **Avoid Blocking the Event Loop:** The most critical rule in `asyncio` is to never perform long-running, synchronous, or CPU-bound operations (like heavy calculations or blocking I/O) directly within an `async` function. Doing so will freeze the entire event loop, preventing other tasks from running. Use `run_in_executor` to offload such tasks to a thread or process pool.
2.  **Use `asyncio.gather` for Concurrency:** Instead of running tasks sequentially, use `asyncio.gather()` to schedule multiple coroutines to run concurrently. This allows you to wait for all of them to complete efficiently, significantly improving performance for I/O-bound operations.
3.  **Properly Manage Task Lifecycles:** Always ensure that tasks are properly awaited or cancelled. Leaving "dangling" tasks that are never awaited can lead to resource leaks and unexpected behavior. Use `asyncio.create_task()` to schedule tasks and ensure they are handled correctly within the event loop's lifecycle.
4.  **Prefer `asyncio.run()`:** For the entry point of your application, use `asyncio.run()` rather than manually managing the event loop (e.g., `loop = asyncio.get_event_loop()`). `asyncio.run()` handles the creation and closing of the loop automatically, which is the recommended, cleaner approach in modern Python.
5.  **Use Timeouts:** When performing network requests or other operations that might hang, always wrap your calls in `asyncio.wait_for()` with a specified timeout. This prevents your application from waiting indefinitely for a resource that may never respond.
